The venture is otherwise where it was: capital-efficient, well-analyzed, structurally capped at a $249M category, and still without a built unit, a signed customer, or a regulatory determination.

**Why L(3) and not higher.** The rubric's L(3) is "solid relevant experience with some industry connections"; XL(5) requires "deep domain expertise with strong industry network and track record." **Confidence is Medium, not High.** Everything here is self-reported and arrived after two scorecards flagged the gap. The advisor agreement is not yet written. None of it is implausible — a commercial tour operation and an autonomous-vessel engineering role are specific, checkable claims — but nothing in the data room corroborates them.

### 3. Customer Urgency — L (3) · Confidence: Low-Medium

**This is the dimension where the new submission promised the most and delivered the least resolvable evidence.**

**What the document claims.** Fifteen buyer discovery conversations across seven site categories, conducted during July 2026 by four founders drawing on their own networks, by phone, in person and over messaging, structured around the fifteen-question guide. Seven of fifteen sites clear the $3,000/month spend threshold. Twelve of fifteen had attempted a permanent build and been blocked — quoted at $150,000 to $1,800,000 and killed by trenching costs, coastal commissions, FEMA flood elevation, a Historic Preservation Board, seagrass protection, and a water authority refusing sewer lines near a drinking reservoir. Nine sites describe money already lost.

If established, that is strong evidence of active search behaviour, which is precisely what the rubric uses to separate "actively seek solutions" from "tolerate workarounds," and it would support XL(5).

**Why it cannot be scored as established.** The predecessor document contains the same fifteen profiles with every dollar figure, every count, and every construction quote identical, and describes them as "constructed personas... **not transcripts of customer interviews**." The current document describes the same content as "**live conversations, not desk research**." The only substantive changes are the entity names — invented site names replaced by segment-and-role descriptors — and two new closing sections.

The team's own methodology note then discloses that "original notes and message threads were lost during a device migration," that no statement should be treated as a verbatim quotation, that dollar figures are "approximate ranges recalled," and that participants did not consent to being named.

**A fair reading of the benign explanation.** Module 7/8/9 is a persona exercise. A team that had genuinely held these conversations could reasonably have written them up under invented site names to satisfy the assignment, attaching a disclaimer that overstated how synthetic the result was — and the rewrite would then be a correction *toward* the truth. That reading is plausible and I cannot rule it out. Neither can I confirm it, and the records that would settle it are gone.

**Scoring consequence.** The framework instruction is explicit: score conservatively where evidence is uncertain, one size below what the evidence might otherwise support. Evidence as claimed supports XL(5); one size lower is L(3), which is also where the identical content scored when it was labelled modeled. The score therefore holds at 3.

**This is the cheapest two points available to this venture.** Five conversations re-run with contemporaneous written notes, a date, a role, and a figure captured at the time would move this dimension to 5 and lift confidence across Cost of Sales and Profitability simultaneously. The interview guide is already excellent. Nothing is blocking this but a fortnight of calls.
