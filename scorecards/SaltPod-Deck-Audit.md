# Salt Pod Pitch Deck: Audit and Fixes Applied

**Deck:** Module 20, eleven slides
**Audited against:** the 21 document data room, August 2026
**Status:** APPLIED. All findings below have been fixed in the deck. Deck is now twelve slides.

---

## The problem in one line

The deck was revised three times while the underlying documents were also being revised, and slide 7 fell a version behind. It now quotes a funnel that Module 16 explicitly retired.

---

## 1. Slide 7 is wrong and must be fixed

**What the slide says:**

> 252 qualified sites · 157 opportunities · 11 units · **62% market touched**
> Header: FLORIDA WATER-ACCESS SITES. FIRST YES WINS.
> "62% is aggressive. Sales capacity is the constraint."

**What Module 16 now says:**

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Opportunities required | ~158 |
| Phase 1 addressable, Tier 1 Southeast | 712 |
| Market touch rate required | **22%** |
| National qualified base | 6,104 |

Module 16 names the deck's own figure as the version it replaced: *"The prior version of this brief required a 62 percent touch rate against a Florida-only base."*

The deck is therefore presenting the superseded, worse number, and framing it as a weakness that no longer exists. This is the only slide in the deck that contradicts a source document, and it does so in the direction that makes the venture look harder to execute than it is.

**Replacement text:**

> Header: **US WATER-ACCESS SITES. TIERED BY PATHWAY. FIRST YES WINS.**
>
> 6,104 qualified sites nationally · 712 Tier 1 Southeast for Phase 1 · 158 opportunities · 11 units · **22% market touched**
>
> Qualified = already spends $3,000+/mo on sanitation.
> Tier 1 = confirmed authorization pathway, four to six weeks.
>
> Speaker note: This replaces the earlier Florida-only funnel, which needed 62 percent of a much smaller base. Widening nationally and tiering by regulatory pathway fixed it without changing the product or the pricing.

---

## 2. The regulatory work is missing entirely, and it is your strongest slide

Four written responses from FDEP's Division of State Lands, three confirmed pathways, and a four to six week route where every competitor in the category assumes twelve to twenty-four months. You established this before spending anything on fabrication.

None of it appears in the deck. Slide 3 says "No permits to chase" as a customer benefit, and slide 11 allocates 10 percent of the raise to "Legal, permitting, insurance." That is all.

This is the clearest evidence in the whole data room that the team can execute something hard, and an investor or examiner reading the deck alone would never know it happened.

**Add a slide, suggested position after slide 4:**

> **Title:** The Permit Nobody Else Asked About
> **Kicker:** WE HOLD WRITTEN AGENCY GUIDANCE
>
> | Pathway | Authorization | Timeline |
> |---|---|---|
> | Alongside a government pier | Letter of consent | **4 to 6 weeks** |
> | Non-sovereignty waterbodies | None required | No process |
> | Anchored on state bottom | Board lease | 12 to 24 months |
>
> Anchor to the bottom and you create new preemption, which forces the lease.
> Secure alongside a pier and you ride an authorization that already exists.
> Same product. Different attachment. Seventeen months of difference.
>
> Our competitors build heavy moored platforms. They are on the slow path by design.
>
> Speaker note: Four written responses from the Division of State Lands, obtained before we committed to fabrication. We also ruled out vessel classification, since Florida law expressly excludes floating structures from the definition. One question is open, whether a private marina pier meets the public access test, and we are filing for a binding determination on it.

That last line matters. It shows you know where your own answer stops.

## 3. Four small fixes

**Slide 5, speaker note.** The note says "before the 79 percent figure." The slide face says 80%. Change the note to 80 percent.

**Slide 10, speaker note.** States the LTV ratio as 9.6 to 1. The figures give 9.26 to 1 ($135,960 divided by $12,100 plus $2,580). Change to 9.3 to 1, or drop the ratio and leave LTV and CAC to speak for themselves.

**Slide 6.** Shows $249M and $1.2B separately but never adds them. State the combined figure, since that is the number that changes how the venture is categorised.

> Add: **Combined addressable market: $1.45B today, $3.0B by 2030.**

**Slide 8.** The Salt Pod row uses an em dash in the Threat column. Replace with "n/a" or leave blank.

## 4. What is already right

Worth not touching:

- **Slide 6** is the strongest addition to the deck. Two lines, two revenue shapes, two markets, and the phase sequence in one view.
- **Slide 8** leads with the real competitive insight rather than a feature table: *"Our real competitor is a build that never got permitted. $150K to $1.8M quoted. All blocked."*
- **Slide 9** describes the team accurately, including the vendor gaps, and names the advisor.
- **Slide 11** says "Next: prototype build. Three documented budget commitments." That is the honest next step and it matches the plan.
- **Slide 4 speaker note** tells the presenter to state plainly that the patent plan was reversed on prior art. Most decks would bury that.

1. **Fix slide 7.** It is the only factual contradiction with a source document.

The deck is eleven slides and would be twelve with the regulatory addition, which is still a reasonable length. If something has to come out to make room, slide 3 carries the least new information, since the value proposition is already implicit in slides 2 and 5.
